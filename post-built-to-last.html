Built to Survive, Not Built to Last

Stop building just to survive today. Build systems that can contain your future capacity.

By Nana Boateng · 2025 · 4 min read

Most African startups don't fail because of bad ideas. They fail because they are built to survive, not built to last.

When I finished university, reality hit hard.

A friend introduced me to graphic design in my final year. After graduation, it became my lifeline. I started solving visual problems, found my passion, and began making money.

But I refused to think small.

When I started my design agency, I immediately built a team. People mocked me: “A graphic designer with a customer service manager?” They didn't understand the vision. I wasn't building a freelance gig. I was building an institution.

Hiring my first team member changed everything. The weight of responsibility shifted. It was no longer just about feeding myself. It was about inspiring someone else to think, do, and execute at a global level.

To transition from survival to scale, I went back to school for my MBA.

Too many brilliant African entrepreneurs are fixated on the immediate present.

  • They make decisions that favor today but kill tomorrow.
  • They lack the confidence to make bold, long-term moves.

And if we don't change this mindset, our continent will continue to lose its most beautiful ideas.

This is why I added “54” to my name. It is my North Star.

Through Sash Afrique Limited, I am out to prove that you can start small in Africa and build a lasting legacy like Nike or Apple.

As a Growth Leader, my current focus isn't just chasing quick sales. We are aggressively investing time and energy into building robust systems that can handle massive capacity.

We are structurally preparing to serve beyond the borders of Ghana.

Stop building just to survive today. Build systems that can contain your future capacity.

Let's change the narrative. Are you building a hustle for today, or an empire for tomorrow?
